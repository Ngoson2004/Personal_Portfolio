## The problem

Screening drug candidates in the lab is slow and expensive. If a model can predict a molecule's pharmacokinetic properties up front, chemists can spend lab time on the candidates most likely to succeed.

## Approach

At CSIRO I developed and evaluated an AI application that predicts these properties, and entered the approach in the OpenADMET and ExpansionRx blind challenge.

- **Graph representations.** Molecules are featurised as graphs and modelled with Chemprop message-passing networks. I also experimented with adding fingerprint features (Morgan, Avalon, ErG and MACCS).
- **Multi-task learning.** The model learns all endpoints jointly, supplemented with public datasets (Galapagos' Polaris set and a merged Novartis set of roughly 300K molecules) after chemical curation and SMILES canonicalisation.
- **Target transforms.** Skewed endpoints are log-transformed, with a logit transform for percentage endpoints.
- **Ensembling.** Models are trained on five random splits and the submission averages all five checkpoints.
- **Tuning at scale.** Training and hyperparameter search ran on HPC with SLURM. Hand-picked hyperparameters ended up beating automated Tree-structured Parzen Estimator search.

## Results

- **Top 13** of 370+ global participants.
- **0.588** macro-averaged relative absolute error.

## Working across disciplines

The work was shared with AI, chemistry and bioengineering teams to judge practical applicability, with findings written up as technical reports for stakeholders.
